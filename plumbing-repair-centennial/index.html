When a plumbing problem needs attention

A Plumbing Repair Centennial call is useful when something in your home isn’t working as it should, such as a leaking pipe, a faulty fixture, or a plumbing connection that has become unreliable. If water is actively escaping, treat it as time-sensitive. Keep people clear of the wet area, and use a nearby shutoff only if you can reach it safely and know how it works.

Notice what is happening without taking equipment apart. Is the issue constant or does it happen only when a fixture is used? Is water visible near a pipe, under a sink, or around a fixture? These observations can help describe the problem when you call.

What to share and what to avoid

A useful description includes the affected fixture or area, when you first noticed the issue, and whether it has changed. Mention any steps you’ve already taken, including shutting off a valve or placing a container to catch drips. If the problem comes and goes, explain when you tend to notice it.

Before a plumber arrives, you can:
- Move belongings away from the affected area.
- Keep children and pets away from standing water.
- Identify the nearest shutoff if you know where it is.
- Take a photo from a safe, dry position if it helps show the issue.

Avoid opening walls, forcing stuck handles, or using tools on a connection you don’t understand. Don’t mix drain products or apply a chemical cleaner if you’re unsure what is already in the line. If you have used a product, tell the plumber what it was.

Questions people ask about Plumbing Repair Centennial

What information should I give when calling about a plumbing repair?

Describe the fixture or area, when the problem started, and whether it happens continuously or only during use. Mention any water shutoff or other action you’ve already taken.

Should I try to repair a leak before calling?

Don’t take apart a connection if you’re unsure how it works. If you can safely reach a nearby shutoff and know how to use it, that may help limit water while you arrange professional help.

Is a small drip worth calling about?

A small drip can still lead to water damage, so it’s sensible to ask for advice rather than ignore it. Explain where it appears and whether it changes when the fixture is used.
